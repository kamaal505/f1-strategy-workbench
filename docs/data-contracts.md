# Snapshot data contract

Snapshots are immutable, content-addressed Parquet publications under `snapshots/<sha256>/`. The mutable FastF1 response cache is separate and is never consulted by replay. The identity includes the schema, session, stream origin, race start, originally scheduled distance, entry and event metadata, source identifiers, acquisition options, availability, quality findings, dependency and lock versions, and each sorted file path, row count and content hash. Acquisition time is an annotation and does not affect identity. The reader validates the manifest identity, event metadata digest, schemas, row counts, file hashes, required streams and confined member paths before exposing read-only Arrow tables. It rejects symlinks beneath the snapshot root. Identical publication reuses an already verified snapshot; a changed input produces a different identity.

## Origins and release

All canonical temporal fields use signed nanoseconds relative to FastF1's session stream origin (`t0_date`). `event_ns` and `release_ns` are nullable; original receipt times are unavailable. A completed lap is released at its estimated end, and pit entry and exit are separate events released at their own estimated times. Weather, track and session status are released at their source timestamps; race-control messages use their UTC message timestamps. Missing timestamps are not imputed. Canonical lap durations and sectors are seconds; lap, stint and tyre-age counts are nullable integers. Driver numbers are strings. Compounds not identified by the source are `UNKNOWN`, not inferred dry compounds. The lap table also retains final deletion and position for audit, not as replay features. Race status and messages are subject to historical corrections.

The original returned source frames are retained separately under `source/`, with columns, order, Arrow-representable types and nulls, not represented as raw live messages. Optional car and position streams are separate, preserve their original channels and time bases, and include canonical sample metadata when available. No telemetry is acquired unless requested. A missing optional stream has explicit availability and reason; missing or empty laps, track status or session status prevents publication. Availability records whether data were actually returned, not merely requested.

Quality findings count duplicate driver/lap pairs, missing, negative or reversed timing, nonpositive lap times, sector-sum mismatches, tyre-age regressions and unmatched pit events. Suspect rows are retained. Telemetry quality records missing channels and median cadence. These checks do not establish sporting legality or make slow laps invalid. Stints used in replay must be constructed from released rows; a final full-race stint summary must not be exposed as a prefix observation.

FastF1's documented `total_laps` is the originally scheduled distance. Exact event-date rules, compound nominations and circuit specification are not inferred from the season. Tyre life may include previous-session use. Neither source subscriptions nor library licensing establishes permission to retain or publicly display data. Acquisition requires explicit operator assertions of source access and snapshot-retention permission; these are not a public-use authorization. Credentials and authentication caches must not be copied into a snapshot. No real acquisition was performed as part of implementing this contract.

## Offline query boundary

Opening a complete snapshot does not import FastF1, contact the network, consult the calendar, authenticate or fall back to the mutable cache. `snapshot_query` creates and closes a request-scoped in-memory DuckDB connection over verified Arrow tables; `prefix_table` parameterizes the cutoff and restricts rows to `release_ns <= cutoff`. A hosting coordinator must acquire a lease before opening a snapshot and hold it through query and payload extraction; query code does not itself implement that coordinator.

Event-time reconstructed replay; historical corrections and original arrival times are not fully observable.
