# Snapshot data contract

Snapshots are immutable, content-addressed Parquet publications under `snapshots/<sha256>/`. The identity includes the schema, session, stream origin, race start, scheduled distance, entry and event metadata, source identifiers, acquisition options, availability, quality findings, dependency and lock versions, and each sorted file path, row count and content hash. Acquisition time is an annotation and does not affect identity. The reader validates the manifest identity, event metadata digest, schemas, row counts, file hashes, required streams and confined member paths before exposing read-only Arrow tables. It rejects symlinks beneath the snapshot root. Identical publications reuse their identity.

## Origins and release

All canonical temporal fields use signed nanoseconds relative to FastF1's session stream origin (`t0_date`). `event_ns` and `release_ns` are nullable; original receipt times are unavailable. A completed lap is released at its estimated end, and pit entry and exit are separate events released at their own estimated times. Weather, track and session status are released at their source timestamps; race-control messages use their UTC message timestamps. Missing timestamps are not imputed. Canonical lap durations and sectors are seconds; lap, stint and tyre-age counts are nullable integers. Driver numbers are strings. Compounds not identified by the source are `UNKNOWN`, not inferred dry compounds. The lap table also retains final deletion and position for audit, not as replay features. Race status and messages are subject to historical corrections.

Original archive JSON is retained byte-for-byte in binary Parquet source payloads under `source/`, including unconsumed fields and literal missing-value markers; these are not raw live messages. Optional per-lap telemetry is processed, merged/interpolated data, not native separate car and position streams. It is acquired only on request, with partial availability and missing-file reasons retained. Its `Time` is slice-relative, without a reliable absolute anchor: sample event/release timestamps remain null, so release-gated replay does not display those samples. Missing or empty laps, track status or session status prevents publication.

Quality findings count duplicate driver/lap pairs, missing, negative or reversed timing, nonpositive lap times, sector-sum and lap-clock/duration mismatches, tyre-age regressions and unmatched pit events. Suspect rows are retained. Telemetry quality records missing channels, null counts and median within-slice cadence per driver/lap. These checks do not establish sporting legality or make slow laps invalid. Stints used in replay must be constructed from released rows; a final full-race stint summary must not be exposed as a prefix observation.

The archive's FastF1-derived `total_laps` is the source scheduled-distance field, not the maximum completed laps. Exact event-date rules, compound nominations and circuit specification are not inferred from the season. Tyre life may include previous-session use. Anonymous availability and code licensing do not establish permission to retain or publicly display F1 data. Hosted acquisition requires separate source-access and snapshot-retention authorization, and public use requires further scopes.

## Offline query boundary

Opening a complete snapshot does not contact the network, consult the calendar, authenticate or fall back to a mutable cache. `snapshot_query` creates and closes a request-scoped in-memory DuckDB connection over verified Arrow tables; `prefix_table` parameterizes the cutoff and restricts rows to `release_ns <= cutoff`. A hosting coordinator must acquire a lease before opening a snapshot and hold it through query and payload extraction; query code does not itself implement that coordinator.

Event-time reconstructed replay; historical corrections and original arrival times are not fully observable.
